MK-IV Robotic Arm

A high-precision six-axis robotic arm designed for micro-assembly in aerospace manufacturing. Engineered for sub-micron accuracy under continuous duty cycles.

FIG. 01 — ASSEMBLY OVERVIEW

Overview

The MK-IV is a six-axis articulated arm built to place components smaller than a grain of rice with repeatable, sub-micron accuracy. It was developed to close the gap between manual micro-assembly and full automation in low-volume aerospace production.

The design prioritises stiffness-to-mass, thermal stability, and serviceability — every joint is a sealed, pre-loaded module that can be swapped in the field without re-calibrating the whole arm.

How it was made

Process

01

Requirements & Concept

Defined the accuracy, payload, and duty-cycle targets with the manufacturing team, then sketched three kinematic layouts and scored them against stiffness, workspace, and serviceability.

IMAGE / SKETCH SLOT
02

CAD & Design

Modelled the full arm in SolidWorks as a parametric assembly, driving joint sizing from a torque budget so link geometry updated automatically as loads were refined.

IMAGE / CAD SLOT
03

Simulation & Analysis

Ran FEA on each link for deflection under worst-case pose and a modal study to keep the first natural frequency clear of the servo bandwidth. Iterated the base casting to cut tip deflection by 34%.

IMAGE / FEA SLOT
04

Prototyping & Machining

CNC-machined the structural links from 7075-T6, 3D-printed cable guides and tooling jigs, and assembled pre-loaded bearing modules on a granite surface plate.

IMAGE / BUILD SLOT
05

Testing & Validation

Verified repeatability with a laser interferometer across the full workspace and ran a 500-hour endurance cycle. Logged results against the spec and fed findings back into the MK-V roadmap.
