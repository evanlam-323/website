Lattice Bracket

A topology-optimised titanium bracket that is 42% lighter than the part it replaces at equivalent yield strength, produced by metal additive manufacturing.

FIG. 01 — OPTIMISED GEOMETRY

Overview

This bracket carries a fixed load path between two mounting faces where every gram matters. Starting from a machined baseline, the project used topology optimisation and metal 3D printing to remove material everywhere it was not doing structural work.

The final organic lattice was validated to the same yield margin as the original while cutting mass by nearly half — and consolidating three fasteners into one printed part.

How it was made

Process

01

Load Cases

Captured the mounting geometry, load paths, and keep-out zones, then defined the design space and non-design regions for optimisation.

IMAGE / SETUP SLOT
02

Topology Optimisation

Ran a mass-minimisation study under the load cases, sweeping volume-fraction targets to find the best strength-to-weight trade-off.

IMAGE / OPTIMISATION SLOT
03

Reconstruction & DFAM

Rebuilt the raw optimisation result into a clean, printable body — adding self-supporting angles and smoothing stress concentrations.

IMAGE / CAD SLOT
04

Metal Printing

Printed in Ti-6Al-4V by DMLS, then stress-relieved, removed from the plate, and finished the mating faces on a mill.

IMAGE / PRINT SLOT
05

Proof Testing

Load-tested the printed bracket to limit and ultimate load, confirming it met the yield margin of the original machined part.
