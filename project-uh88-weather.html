UH 88″ Deployable Weather Sensor

At the Institute for Astronomy, I designed a deployable mechanism that extends a Boltwood Cloud Sensor out over the shutter of the UH 88-inch telescope on Maunakea — reading sky conditions in real time, then stowing clear so the dome can safely close.

FIG. 01 — DEPLOYABLE SENSOR SYSTEM

Overview

Maunakea is one of the harshest observing sites on Earth — 60+ mph winds, freezing rain, and heavy ice buildup. Telescopes rely on roof-mounted weather sensors to decide when to close, but the summit's previous cloud sensor was lost to those conditions, and a fixed sensor can foul the dome as it opens and closes.

My project solves that: a deployable sensor arm that drives a Boltwood Cloud Sensor II — plus an HQ camera — out past the telescope railings to take a clean reading, then retracts fully out of the shutter's path.

Fail-safe design

Safety Features

Shear Pin

An M5 pin ties the rail housing to the frame — if the shutter strikes it, the pin snaps and the rails swing clear of the opening.

Current Limit

The motor controller caps drive current so a jam can't overload the mechanism or the summit's low-voltage supply.

Limit Switch

A homing switch establishes the zero point automatically and bounds travel at the retracted end.

Fuse

An inline fuse protects the electronics stack against fault currents from the telescope's 120 V feed.
