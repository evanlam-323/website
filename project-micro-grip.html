Surgical Micro-Grip

Compliant-mechanism forceps for minimally invasive procedures — a single-piece flexure grip with no pins or hinges to trap fluid or fail, designed to ISO 13485 practice.

FIG. 01 — FLEXURE GRIP

Overview

Traditional surgical forceps rely on tiny pinned joints that are hard to clean and prone to wear. This project replaced the mechanism with a monolithic compliant flexure — the whole grip flexes as one part, with no assembly, no crevices, and predictable pinch force.

Design and documentation followed ISO 13485 quality practice, with a full design-history file, biocompatible material selection, and fatigue-life validation.

Process

01

Clinical Requirements

Worked from the surgical use-case to set target pinch force, tip travel, cleanability, and sterilisation constraints.

IMAGE / RESEARCH SLOT
02

Flexure Design

Designed a compliant mechanism that converts handle input into jaw motion through living hinges, sizing beams for the required force and travel.

IMAGE / CAD SLOT
03

Nonlinear FEA

Ran large-deflection FEA to keep peak stress below the fatigue limit through the full grip stroke, iterating hinge thickness for a smooth force curve.

IMAGE / FEA SLOT
04

Prototyping

Printed functional PEEK prototypes to feel the ergonomics, then wire-EDM cut a 316L version for mechanical testing.

IMAGE / BUILD SLOT
05

Fatigue Testing

Cycled the grip on a test rig past 100k actuations while monitoring force retention, and compiled results into the design-history file.
