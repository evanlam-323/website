Exoskeleton Heat-Sink

Passive thermal management for wearable robotic exoskeletons operating under continuous load, keeping actuator temperatures within safe limits without active cooling.

FIG. 01 — FIN ARRAY

Overview

Wearable exoskeletons dump a surprising amount of heat into a small, enclosed volume next to the wearer. This project delivered a lightweight passive heat-sink that spreads and rejects actuator heat while staying comfortable against the body.

The solution combined a topology-optimised fin array with a heat-spreading baseplate, tuned entirely for natural convection so the system carries no fans, pumps, or added failure points.

How it was made

Process

01

Thermal Budget

Measured actuator dissipation across a typical gait cycle and set a maximum skin-contact temperature to design against.

IMAGE / DATA SLOT
02

Concept & CAD

Modelled several fin geometries and a copper-cored spreader baseplate, parameterising fin pitch and height for later optimisation.

IMAGE / CAD SLOT
03

CFD Simulation

Ran natural-convection CFD to compare candidates, converging on a fin array that maximised surface area without choking airflow between fins.

IMAGE / CFD SLOT
04

Prototyping

Machined the baseplate, bonded the copper core, and assembled the fin stack with thermal interface material for bench testing.

IMAGE / BUILD SLOT
05

Bench Validation

Loaded the sink with a resistive heater and logged steady-state temperatures with thermocouples, confirming the 28 °C reduction target.
